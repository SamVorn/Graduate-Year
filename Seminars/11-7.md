# Crowdsourced Enforcement of Access Rights in Shared Spectrum Networks
TWO CONFLICTING TRENDS
- static spectrum allocation
    - traditional spectrum allocation policy grants fixed spectrum band to its licensed users for exclusive access
- spectrum scarcity
    - inability to meet exponentially rising demands of mobile and wireless servicces despite the existance of idle spectrurm across many statically allocated bands


A viable solution
- shared spectrum access allows spectrum to be allocated dynamically to different users while enforcing access rights
    - enhances spectrum utilization
    - requires management of interference
    - possibility of spectrum access vilation and misuse by intruders
        - this gives rise to the need for spectrum access rights protection in shared spectrum networks
            - citizens brodband radio service
            - three tiered access right infrastructure
            1. incumbnets - navy saltelites etc
            2. pal - internet service providers
            3. gaa - general devices, phones laptops, etc
        - whenever a tear is using a spectrum band, the other tiers are not, depends on who is given priority

## spectrum enforcement classification
- ex ante (before the fact) enforcement
    - before a potentially harmful action has occured
- ex post (after the fact) enforcement
    - enforcement after a potentially harmful action has occured

## Ex post main approach
- traditional methods
    - deployment of dedicated, expensive static monitoring infrastructure
    - deasigned primarily to manage interferance, when spectrum sharing is allowed
        - need to integrate malicious spectrum use by intruders and rogue users
        - suppose a ddos attack or other issues - need to deal withthose
    - not cost effective to achieve coverage over large enforcement areas

- crowdsourcing 
    - high potential to achieve cost effectiveness, at scale

Research statement
- 
- crowdsourcing is a viable approach to develop a robust framework for cost effective spectrum access rights enforcement

## driving questions
- how can cost effective spectrum coverage be achieved reliably over an extended geographical area
- how can crowdsourced spectrum nonitoring agents be selected to ensure full coverage and accurate detection of misuse
- how can robust spectrum monitoring be achieved efficiently

## enforcement structure
- enforcement is carried out over monitoring intervals of fixed size
    - volunteers are selected at the beginning of every mi
- each mi is divided into working unit intervals WUIs
    - a set of access slots
    - smallest interval over which a user, intruder, or legitimate can accomplish useful work
- an access slot AS is a timeslot over which a sentinel or a volunteer senses the cahnnel to monitor access

Selection goals and process

Volunteer selection criteria
- trustworthiness (reputation)
- in region availability and likelihood
    - likelihood to visit the region
        - total amount of time spent in the target region relative to others
    - region arrival time interval
        - estimated interval of time before a volunteer reaches the target region
    - sojourn time
        - time volunteer is expected to spend within the target region

Used to calculate the volunteer overall qualification

Volunteer selection algorithm
- objective
    - given a set of volunteers V every volunteer v has a qualification qv
        - the objective is to ensure that the highest probability of selecting the best k volunteers is achieved
            - maximizing the expected payoff

objective
- spectrum monitoring strategy should ensure successful detection of spectrum access violation against
    - intruders of different profiles
    - corrupt volunteers
    - sensing failures
- needs to be robust enough to detect all violations

monitoring strategy
- volunteers
    - monitor enough number of times such that an intruder is caught
- sentinels
    - monitor enough number of times such that a corrupt volunteer is caught

managing corrupt volunteers
- utilizes a volunteer reputation management scheme
- basic tenet of the approach
    - update repuatation based on volunteer performance
    - to increase the reputation slowly after success
    - to penalize the reputation rapidly upon false reporting
- volunteers are expelled when their reputation falls below a threshold
- descrepancy tolerance
    - prevent expulsion of honest volunteers by mistake

results - descrepancy tolerance sensitivity
- decision on choosing descrepancy tolerance to avoid eviction of an honest volunteer
